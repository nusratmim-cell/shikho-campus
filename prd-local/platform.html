Overview · How everything connects

How everything connects

The module documents describe features one at a time. This page describes what happens between them — the chains of consequence that make a university run, and the outside systems we depend on.

Live demo Open the live campus demo and try any role. shikho-brac-platform.vercel.app ↗

The one idea

A university is a chain of consequences. Attendance decides exam eligibility. Exam eligibility decides who appears on the mark sheet. The mark sheet decides the grade. The grade decides progression. Payment decides registration. Registration decides class lists.

In most universities these links are made by a person carrying information from one office to another — a printed list, an email, a phone call. Each hand-off is a place where things get delayed, lost or disputed.

In Shikho Campus the link is made by the system itself. When a teacher saves attendance, the student's eligibility figure changes in the same moment, on the student's phone, on the teacher's register, and on the chairperson's eligibility screen. Nobody carries anything.

Why this matters commercially A university can buy a video tool, a quiz tool and an accounting tool separately. What they cannot buy separately is the chain. The chain is the product.

Seven roles, not four

The product has four modules (Student, Faculty, Administrator, Coordinator). People hold roles as dated appointments. A person can hold several at once; the interface switches context — it does not become a different product. Teaching assistant, advisor and research assistant are first-class; version-one wireframes that skipped them were incomplete.

Control rules (how settings behave)

Configurable settings live in Overview · What each university can change. Three rules apply to every one of them:

  1. Nothing is retroactive. A change carries the term (or date) it starts from. Anything already computed under the old value keeps it — that is what makes an old transcript defensible years later.
  2. Every change previews its impact. Before saving, the screen states how many students, sections or invoices the new value would touch. Live calendar moves that affect running terms need a second confirmer and a signed history entry.
  3. The narrowest scope wins. Department overrides university; a section’s marks distribution sits inside department bounds; each screen shows which level a value came from.

If a rule is not in the settings list, it is hard-coded and needs engineering. Permission codes for who may change what: RBAC.

Chain 2 · Money and holds

Finance decides the amount → Coordinator syncs it → Student sees the bill → Student pays (bKash / Nagad / Rocket / card / bank slip) → University's account receives the money → confirmation arrives → Receipt issued → hold clears → blocked action becomes available
QuestionAnswer
Who decides how much a student owes?The university's finance office. The platform only mirrors it.
Where does the money go?Straight into the university's own account through a licensed payment provider. It never passes through us.
What if the student's internet drops mid-payment?The bill page shows "checking with the bank" and resolves itself when confirmation arrives. Paying twice for the same bill is prevented.
What if money left the wallet but nothing updated?The student raises it with the payment reference; the coordinator matches it manually and the reason is recorded.
What can unpaid dues block?Whatever the university chooses — registration, exam entry, seeing results, getting a transcript, and, where the university turns it on, access to classes and new content — each set separately as a hard block, a warning, or nothing.
Can unpaid tuition really stop a student attending class?Yes, if the university enables the access gate — because an unpaid student is not truly enrolled. But it is deliberately humane: the student is warned days ahead, keeps what they have already earned, sees the exact amount and a one-tap pay, is never removed from a class in progress, and can have the gate lifted by an advisor or finance for an agreed payment date.
Who can waive a due?Nobody in the platform. A waiver is decided in finance or by the scholarship committee and arrives as a changed amount.
What about students who pay at a bank counter?They upload the slip, the coordinator verifies it, and from that point everything behaves exactly like an online payment.

Chain 3 · From attendance to the exam hall

Class held → teacher saves the register (joining a live class marks presence automatically) → student's attendance figure updates immediately → student sees "you may miss 2 more classes" → advisor sees an at-risk flag if the student is close to the line → before finals the chair reviews everyone below the line → chair grants exceptions with reasons, then locks eligibility → barred students are removed from the exam list and told why
RuleBehaviour
The student always knows where they standAttendance is never a surprise at the end of term. The number and the remaining allowance are visible from week one.
Disputes are handled with evidenceEvery attendance record shows who marked it, when, and every later change.
Exceptions are allowed but never invisibleMedical and official-duty exemptions require a reason and are attached to the student's record permanently.
Once locked, the list is stableAfter locking, changing one student's eligibility is a separate, recorded decision — not an edit.

Chain 4 · From a mark to a transcript

Student submits → teacher marks (AI may draft, teacher approves) → teacher posts marks → student sees them → teacher submits the final grade sheet → sheet locks for the teacher → chair certifies, or returns it with a reason → certified results go to the registrar → results published to students → any later change is an amendment with a reason and approval
SafeguardWhy it exists
Only the teacher gives marksAcademic judgement belongs to the person who taught the course. Nothing in the administrator's screens can change a mark.
The chairperson can only certify or returnGovernance is approval, not editing. Returning a sheet requires a written reason that the teacher sees.
AI never grades aloneSuggested marks and feedback are drafts. The teacher's approval is what makes them real, and the record shows what the AI proposed and what the teacher changed.
Nothing disappearsEvery version of a mark is kept. A student asking "why did my grade change?" can be answered from the record.
Corrections after publication are formalA published grade can be amended, but only as a new approved decision with a reason attached.

Bangladesh reality the product is designed around

RealityWhat we do about it
Most students use a mid-range Android phone on mobile dataEvery essential task works on a phone. Video quality drops automatically instead of freezing. Recordings can be downloaded for offline watching.
Electricity and internet drop without warningQuiz answers and attendance survive a disconnection. A student returning after a drop resumes where they left off, and the teacher can see it happened.
Mobile wallets are the normal way to paybKash, Nagad and Rocket are first-class payment options, not an afterthought behind card payment.
Many students still pay at a bank counterBank slip upload and verification is a proper feature with the same outcome as an online payment.
Attendance rules are strictly enforced and often disputedAttendance is transparent to the student from day one, and every record carries its own history.
Accreditation visits demand paper evidenceEvidence is produced from the real records already in the system, not assembled by hand at the last minute.
Personal data protection law appliesData stays where the university requires, students can see what is held about them, and access is limited by role.

Term phases — one frame, state changes

A term moves setup → current → closed. Administrator and Coordinator homes keep the same three-part rhythm (Needs you · Timeline · Records/Performance) and change content by phase — not three different pages. Publishing the term, certifying grades, and exporting to the registrar are the hand-offs between phases.
