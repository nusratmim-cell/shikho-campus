Shikho Campus

The AI university learning platform for BRAC University — where four roles, built on a real department's real data, become one product.

For engineering · design · QA · product · sales · leadership Read only this and you can own the product Status High-fidelity product definition

·How to read this

This is a product document — not a technical specification, not marketing. It defines what the product is, why it exists, and how every part must behave. Implementation detail appears only where it clarifies behaviour, at the level needed and no lower.

Understanding is meant to compound. Part I establishes why the product exists and the principles that govern every later decision. Part II defines the foundations every screen inherits. Part III documents each role and surface. Part IV defines the AI. Part V documents the workflows that cross roles. Part VI covers operations and the road ahead. Where a decision could have gone more than one way, this document explains why the chosen way is correct — that reasoning is the most valuable thing here.

Every sentence should increase clarity. If a sentence did not, it was removed.

Part I

The Product

1Vision

Shikho Campus is the learning platform for a university that behaves like a living system rather than a filing cabinet.

A university department is not a database of records. It is a living organism: students learning, faculty teaching, a chair governing, coordinators keeping the machine supplied. Every part depends on every other. Most academic software ignores this. It stores rows. It makes each role log into a different, disconnected tool. It treats a grade as a cell to be edited rather than a decision to be certified.

Shikho Campus is built on the opposite belief: the same reality, seen from four vantage points, should be one product. A student's attendance is the chair's eligibility ruling. A teacher's submitted grades are the chair's certification queue. A coordinator's loaded roster is what the whole term runs on. When these are one system, the university stops fighting its tools and starts running on them.

The product is built by Shikho, a Bangladeshi education company, as the flagship deliverable in its bid to become the technology platform for BRAC University. It is grounded in the real Computer Science & Engineering department: 267 real faculty, 26 real courses, the real 70%-attendance rule, the real trisemester calendar. When an evaluator opens it, they find themselves in the faculty list and their own curriculum on the screen. That is the thesis made literal: software should be built on your reality, not a vendor's abstraction.

2Mission

Give every person in a department the one surface that makes their job obvious — and make those four surfaces provably the same product.

  • The student should feel personally known and gently propelled — never surveilled, never reduced to a rank.
  • The faculty member should feel that AI does the drafting and they do the deciding — always.
  • The administrator should govern the department without ever fabricating or manually assembling a number.
  • The coordinator should feel that the clerical machine is calm, fast, and always handing off cleanly to the people who decide.

3Product principles

These are constraints, not slogans. Every screen, number, and animation obeys them. When a future decision is unclear, resolve it here.

3.1 · Every number traces to a real input

Nothing on any screen is invented. A pass rate is computed from real marks; an eligibility count from real attendance; a department figure is an aggregation of the same section data a teacher already sees. If a number cannot be traced to a real input, it is cut or honestly labelled — never fabricated to look impressive.

Why

Trust is the entire product. A registrar who catches one invented figure never trusts the platform again. Integrity of data is not a feature; it is the foundation the sale rests on.

3.2 · Gamify effort, never grades

The student's motivation system — Momentum — measures consistency of effort. It never gamifies the grade. There is no leaderboard. A student competes only with their own last week.

Why

Gamifying grades produces anxiety and gaming. Gamifying effort produces habits. And a private motivation must never become a public ranking — the moment it does, it stops motivating and starts shaming.

3.3 · The AI drafts; the human decides

Every AI output that touches a record or a student's mind is grounded (answers only from real material, cites or defers) or human-approved (a teacher reviews before a student sees it) — usually both. These guardrails are not toggles a user can disable; they are shown as Locked.

Why

An AI platform in a computer-science department is interrogated by the smartest possible skeptics. The only durable answer is architectural: the AI is structurally incapable of inventing a grade or publishing an unreviewed answer.

3.4 · The administrator certifies; they never author

A chair reviews the shape of submitted grades and certifies to the registrar, or returns for revision. A chair never edits a mark. Every decision is logged.

Why

The integrity of a grade comes from the person who assigned it. Governance means ruling on process, not overwriting outcomes.

3.5–3.9 · The rest of the doctrine

  • Oversight is aggregate, not surveillant. A teacher's private Momentum is never visible to the administrator. Watching people is not the same as governing a department.
  • Govern and operate are different jobs. Decisions belong to the chair; clerical operations belong to the coordinator. Neither role is cluttered with the other's work.
  • One object, one design. The same object — directory, offering, profile, term cockpit — is drawn by one shared component with role-appropriate affordances passed in. Consistency is achieved by architecture, not vigilance.
  • Deterministic and honest about time. The world is pinned to Friday, 10 July 2026, 14:20. No randomness or live clock in any render path.
  • The aesthetic doctrine. Apple-clean · Duolingo-alive · Whoop-personal · registrar-grade. Anything not instantly self-explanatory is killed.

8Identity & permissions

The two teacher identities — subtle but load-bearing

There are two notions of "teacher." Confusing them breaks the product.

  • The wired demo teacher — Dr. Kamrul Hasan. He owns the sample data. All teaching data is filtered by his identity. This never changes.
  • The active ("signed-in-as") teacher. On welcome you may sign in as any of the 267. This changes only the displayed identity — name, title, email, avatar. It never re-filters the data.
Rule

Never filter teaching data by the active identity. The faculty experience always renders the shared sample term; your chosen identity rides on top. This is why an evaluator can "be" any professor and still see a complete class.

The honest grain

The department speaks in two grains: registry scale (≈6,113 enrolled, 148 sections — institution-owned inputs) and graded grain (162 students, ~166 rows — the only place real marks exist). Where they meet, the product explains the gap rather than papering over it. This honesty demonstrates that the platform grows as courses come online, and never pretends to data it lacks.

10Design system

Colour is CSS-variable-backed neutrals, brand tints, and semantics — so dark mode is real (a class, not a filter). Every surface is verified in both themes. Academic years carry a gradient crest (Year 1 indigo → Year 4 coral). Type is a geometric sans; motion is purposeful and calm; a shared reveal animates content in on mount.

The shared component library — the moat against drift

The product is assembled from shared components so that "one object, one design" is structural. The administrator and coordinator directories are the same Directory; their offerings the same Offering; their homes the same Cockpit primitives; all four profiles the same ProfileKit; both faculty pickers the same FacultyPicker; all three AI surfaces the same CampusAI lockup. Two bespoke pages always diverge; one shared component cannot.

The cn caution — a recurring trap

The class-merging helper is plain concatenation, not Tailwind-merge. A later utility does not override an earlier one of the same property. Map to the correct variant; never rely on last-class-wins. This has caused real bugs (a corrupted ring mask on a timeline node) and is a permanent review checkpoint.

Avatars & the photo model

Avatars are deterministic illustrated portraits derived from a name — same person, same face everywhere, no randomness (so no hydration drift). Faculty use monogram tiles. Any user may upload a real photo: downscaled and centre-cropped in the browser, it overrides the illustrated avatar everywhere that identity appears at once — profile hero, ID card, header — through one shared store. Removing it returns the face. One upload, consistent everywhere, instant, removable — that behaviour is the requirement.

11The deterministic-data doctrine

The demo world is a single, internally consistent moment — a hard constraint with a product purpose.

  • Pinned clock: Friday, 10 July 2026, 14:20 — makes CSE110 "live now" for student and teacher, and frames the administrator's home at end-of-term.
  • No Math.random, Date.now, or argless new Date() in any render or data path. Variation comes from a stable hash and fixed anchors.
  • Client-only reads (theme, active teacher, uploaded photo) are gated behind mount effects, so the server and first client render match.

The payoff: the simulation never contradicts itself, and a demo is identical every time it is shown.

12–13Notifications & search

Notifications are role-scoped and reinforce the cross-role loops. The coordinator's and administrator's notifications form a chain — submit (Aug 22) → certify (Aug 28) → post (Sep 1) — with dates that agree across the product. Notifications never surprise; they narrate the term. A global ⌘K search spans the current persona's world — a shortcut, never the only path — and renders above all content.

Part III

Roles & surfaces

Every screen, documented. For each area: purpose, mental model, behaviour, states, validation, permissions, empty states, edge cases, success criteria, extensibility — at the depth the surface warrants.

14The Student

The question the product answers at every moment: "What should I do next, and am I keeping pace?" The mental model is a personal cockpit — my classes, my momentum, my next action. Philosophy: Whoop-personal. The data is intimate and about you. Gamify effort, never grades. Never a leaderboard.

Home/student

The single glance that orients the day: identity line with live chips ("Live now · CSE110," "N due today"), a Momentum summary, a live-class card (join in one tap) when a class is live, a jump-back-in card for the last recording, enrolled courses, and a right rail with today's rings and the tutor.

States Live-now vs. no-class-live · rings incomplete vs. all closed (bonus). Empty A quiet home centred on momentum — never blank. Success A student knows within two seconds whether something needs them now.

Momentum/student/momentum

The private motivation system — the emotional heart of the student product. Measures how consistently you keep pace across your four courses, rolled over two weeks. Effort, never grade. Yours alone, never a leaderboard. A green hero (deep and legible in both themes), a seven-day trend, "how it works," and today's three rings — Absorb, Retrieve, Apply — that reset at midnight. Any one ring keeps the streak; all three earn an XP bonus.

Edge A missed day reduces but never zeroes the score; the streak survives on any single closed ring. Success The student feels propelled, never judged or ranked.

Course detail/student/course/[id]

Everything about one course: content, syllabus (opens in a modal), discussions, grades, and the recorded-lecture player — a synced, searchable transcript, clips, a Smart Class AI panel grounded in that segment's transcript, and autoplay to the next lesson.

Empty A course with no recordings shows a warm "ready to be filled" state, not a void.

Grades/student/grades

Where the student stands, calm: cumulative GPA vs. 4.00, Dean's-List standing, a GPA-trend chart, credits and progress to graduation, a term switcher, and per-course cards with a grade ring and breakdown (final withheld until it exists).

Edge (QA) The GPA-trend renders in a real browser but not under headless capture — which is why product screenshots use the teacher gradebook's CSS distribution. Users are unaffected.

Practice · Tutor/student/practice · /student/tutor

Practice is adaptive AI quizzing that targets weakest topics first. Tutor is the student's mode of Shikho Campus AI — a course-scoped grounded chat that cites the material or defers; it never invents. Both carry the Locked guardrail.

Schedule · Live/student/schedule · /student/live

Schedule renders the real timetable, pinned to the demo clock. Live is the class experience — broadcast stage, attendance, adaptive quality, raise-hand, chat/poll. When no class is live, the surface is honest: it shows the next scheduled class rather than faking a session.

Behaviour (fixed) The chat auto-scrolls within its own pane, never by scrolling the page — which would clip the video hero on the stacked layout.

Profile · Settings · Usage/student/{profile,settings,usage}

Profile (shared ProfileKit): a BRAC Student ID card with the change-photo control. Settings: SSO account (no password), stateful notification toggles, and Learning & AI where Grounded answers only is Always on — locked, by design. Usage: own AI consumption vs. a weekly cap, with the promise you are never cut off mid-lesson.

15The Faculty

The question: "What needs my judgement, and how do I teach with AI without surrendering control?" The mental model is a teaching cockpit where AI drafts and the teacher decides — every mark, every published question.

Grading — the human-in-the-loop flagship/teacher/grading

Grade a queue of student work with AI assistance, approving every mark. Stated on the surface: "The decision is always the teacher's. AI runs the code tests and drafts feedback — no grade reaches a student without your approval." Code items are auto-tested with an AI-drafted score; essays carry a rubric suggestion. The teacher reviews, edits, approves. Nothing publishes unreviewed.

States needs_review → approved. Validation An AI score is a draft; the teacher's approval is the authoritative event.

Gradebook/teacher/gradebook

The class at a glance, drillable by section: course tabs, section filter, a class summary (median, average, pass rate, at-risk), a per-section breakdown, and a grade distribution as CSS bars. Export CSV downloads a real, Excel-friendly file — the one genuinely functional data export.

Copilot/teacher/copilot

The teacher's mode of Shikho Campus AI, grounded in the teacher's own material (RAG over slides, recordings, transcripts). Generates quizzes and lecture notes as drafts the teacher edits and approves before publishing. Guardrail — grounded, you approve — present and locked.

Live · Record/teacher/live · /teacher/record

Live is the broadcast experience (Green Room → live), the mirror of the student's class. Record is a studio: it captures a class, saves it to the course library, and publishes to any tagged curriculum topics — no students are notified (an intentional quiet publish). An untagged recording is simply a general session for that course; the surface never implies a missing action.

Home · Momentum · Courses · Profile · Schedule · Usage · Student detail/teacher/…

Home orients the teaching day. Momentum is private by law — never visible to the administrator. Courses are filtered by the wired demo teacher's identity (see §8). Profile (ProfileKit): a Faculty ID card, this term's stats, courses taught, teaching history across the trisemester. Student detail is a read view of one student's standing.

16The Administrator

The question: "What needs my decision right now, and is the department healthy — without my ever fabricating a number?" The mental model is a cockpit: decisions first, the term as a pipeline, performance explorable in place. She governs; she does not operate; she never authors a mark.

Design decision — one frame, state changes

The home is one frame whose state changes, not three pages per term phase. Whether the term is setup, current, or closed, the home shows the same three sections — Needs you · Timeline · Performance — with phase-appropriate content. Switching terms keeps the same view. This is why the administrator never feels lost.

Home cockpit/chair

Needs you (current): three grounded decision cards — Certify final grades, Finals eligibility, Staff a section (the real unstaffed gap). Closed → "nothing needs you." Setup → "compose the offering · publish." Timeline: the closing term as a pipeline from real deadlines, with certification marked "your step now." Performance: a worst-first, explorable-in-place panel with a Courses / Teachers / Students lens; every row drills to a record; a fixed footprint so switching never lurches. An Ask bar opens the Analyst.

Edge (honest) Real pass rates cluster high (≈97–100%), so "worst first" may lead with 97% — and is labelled as such.

Certify — the flagship governance act/chair/approvals

Certify submitted grades to the registrar, or return them. Promise on the surface: "You never change a mark. Every decision is logged." Each section card shows median, pass rate, delta vs. course average, a distribution histogram, and any flags (attendance anomaly, below-norm distribution). Actions: Certify to registrar or Return.

States submitted → certified / returned. Grounding The queue is derived entirely from the two real gradebooks (the five sections of CSE110 + CSE321). Permission Editing a mark is impossible here by design.

Eligibility/chair/reports

Rule on the BRAC 70%-attendance finals rule. A hero ("N of M clear to sit finals"), three bands (Eligible ≥75 / Approaching 70–74 / Barred <70 · appeals), a worst-first "students on the line" list with Warn + drill, and Lock eligibility (freezes the roster before finals; reversible until they begin). Every flag is a real student from real attendance; the admin rules on exceptions — she never invents the list.

Program/chair/program

Three tabs, one calm frame: This term (compose the offering — staff, seats, add/remove sections, publish), Curriculum (the 26-course catalogue + OBE), Policy (the 70% threshold, the grade scale, term deadlines). Editing policy applies term-wide and is logged; detail expands on demand, never a spreadsheet at rest.

Directory/chair/people

The admin's mode of the shared Directory: Faculty & Students, searchable, with scope filters (Teaching now / Full directory; With records / All enrolled). Faculty grouped by rank, students by course. Every row drills to a read-only scorecard. The footer acknowledges: records maintained by the coordinator.

Analyst / Ask — the killer feature/chair/ask

Ask the department anything; get an answer computed from real records. One uniform Answer Card for every question (headline, stat, ranked rows, source, drill, note) — adding a question is one compute function, never a bespoke screen. A grounded engine with ten intents (worst-attendance, lowest-marks, top-marks, at-risk, weakest-course, eligibility-barred, certification-pending, teacher-load, term-compare, department-summary). A free-text scorer routes the question; no match returns a graceful defer + suggestion chips. Every answer cites its source; nothing is invented; it is read-only.

Why the wow The administrator asks a natural question and the department's own reality answers, instantly, without a report being built. Governance-grade intelligence — grounded, cited, read-only.

Drill-throughs · Profile · Usage/chair/{course,student,faculty}/[id] · /chair/{profile,usage}

Read-only, context-aware look-throughs — every catalogue course opens a real page (sections, staffing, results, or an honest "not offered this term"). A context-aware back control retraces the true origin and restores scroll, so back never dead-ends. Profile (ProfileKit): a Chairperson ID card. Usage: the department AI roll-up — the sum of every capped user, with the "capped, not surprised" story.

17The Coordinator

The question: "Are the records ready, and what must I hand to whom?" The mental model is a records desk and a clean set of hand-offs. She operates; she does not decide. No AI mode (clerical, by design) and no academic authority — faculty assignment and results are the administrator's.

Design decision

The coordinator home is a cockpit that mirrors the administrator's — the same three-part rhythm and the same shared primitives — so the two roles are provably one product. Not a thinner, different page; the same design expressing a different job.

Records home/coordinator

Phase-aware via the shared term switcher. Term prep (current): Flag to the Chair (the unstaffed section — she surfaces it, the chair assigns), Export to registrar (the Sep-1 deadline), Records loaded. Closed: "certified and posted on <date>." Setup: "loading the rosters & offering." Pipeline: the same three deadlines the admin sees, but her "your step" sits on Export while certification is "awaiting the Chair." Records: three import/export desks; import genuinely appends and confirms; export downloads a real CSV; the registry-vs-loaded gap is stated honestly.

Why the switcher must work A control that looks interactive but is not is a broken promise. Hers is the real shared component; her cockpit adapts per phase — because consistency across roles is a principle.

Directory/coordinator/directory

The coordinator's mode of the shared Directory — identical header, search, segmentation, and grouping (faculty by rank, students by course) as the admin's — with maintenance affordances: a New / Import / Export toolbar, per-row edit and delete, per-group and bulk selection with a bulk action bar. Changes are session-local ("saved to this session only"). One add/edit form is built from the same field configuration that validates an import.

Validation Required fields enforced; identity fields locked when editing and uniqueness-checked on create ("already in the directory," Add disabled); rank is a controlled dropdown.

Offering · Profile/coordinator/offering · /coordinator/profile

Offering: the coordinator's mode of the shared Offering — the identical rendering the admin edits, here read + import/export. Imported sections are always left unstaffed, because staffing is the administrator's decision. Footer: faculty assignment and publishing are the Chair's. Profile (ProfileKit): full parity — a Coordinator ID card, a records-desk stat grid (Students, Faculty, Sections, Unstaffed — the operations counterpart to the admin's Pass-rate tile), and a role card naming her reporting line to the Chair.

Part IV

Shikho Campus AI

18–21The AI

One AI product, three modes, one brand lockup, one usage model. The mode always sits last in navigation, in the same slot, for every role. The message to the university is singular: AI you can trust and afford.

The three modes

  • Tutor (student). Course-scoped grounded chat; cites or defers. Powers adaptive Practice and the transcript-grounded Smart Class panel.
  • Copilot (teacher). Grounded in the teacher's material; drafts quizzes, notes, grading feedback; everything reviewed and approved before a student sees it.
  • Analyst (administrator). The grounded query engine over the department's real records (§16). Cite-or-defer; read-only.

The guardrails — shown as Locked

  1. Grounded answers only. Answers only from real material; cites it or says it is not covered. Never invents.
  2. Human review required. Any AI output that reaches a student passes through a teacher's approval.
  3. Administrator integrity. The Analyst is read-only; certifies-not-authors; every drill is logged; a teacher's private momentum is never exposed.
Why usage is a first-class surface

A procurement office's first fear about AI is an unbounded bill. Every user has a weekly cap; usage rolls up; the administrator sees the department total. "Capped, not surprised" — students are never cut off mid-lesson; grounded answers cost a fraction of open-ended generation. This is the "AI you can afford" half of the pitch, and it answers the fear before it is asked.

Part VI

Operations & the road ahead

27Hosting

A Next.js 14 (App Router) application deployed to Vercel at shikho-brac-platform.vercel.app. The production build must remain type-clean — there is no error-suppression in the build config, because a system of record must not ship on a build allowed to lie about its own correctness. Deploys are verified by health-checking live routes and metadata assets.

28Prototype status — stated plainly, because honesty is the product

This document defines the product as it must be. The current build is a high-fidelity, front-end-only prototype — the definitive demonstration that this product can exist and should be built.

  • No backend, database, authentication, or real LLM. Data is deterministic mock; "auth" is a role picker; the AI is grounded, canned, and animated to demonstrate the exact intended behaviour.
  • CSV export is genuinely functional (real files). Photo upload is genuinely functional (persists locally, updates every avatar at once). Theme and identity persist locally.
  • Everything else resets on reload, by contract.

Where the platform is still a prototype (hosting at scale, live SSO, SIS integration, a real model), it is positioned as designed-for / the next step — never as done. This honesty is not a weakness in the pitch; it is the reason the pitch is believed.

29Quality bar — for the incoming Head of Engineering

Held to a world-class visual and interaction bar, verified in light and dark across every persona. Two defect classes are specifically hunted and must stay fixed:

  • Stacking bugs. The header/content/modal z-ordering law (§7). Any element with a transform creates a stacking context and must obey it.
  • False affordances. Nothing may look interactive and do nothing. Every toggle is stateful; every close control works; every "switcher" switches. (A gradient-header close control must sit above the header's content layer, or the transparent content swallows the click — a real, fixed bug; audit every modal against it.)

The definitive "clean" check is a fresh dev-server restart. Retained console errors from rapid edits are not authoritative — the server's compile log, the type-checker, and whether the page renders are.

It must honour every principle in Part I. A backend that let the administrator edit a mark, or an LLM that invented an answer, is a regression no matter how advanced.

C · The one-page essence

Shikho Campus is one product seen from four vantage points — student, faculty, administrator, coordinator — built on a real department's real data. Every number traces to a real input. The AI drafts; the human decides. The administrator certifies but never authors. Effort is gamified; grades never are; nothing is a leaderboard. The same object is drawn by one shared component in every role. Time is pinned so the world is always coherent. It is Apple-clean, Duolingo-alive, Whoop-personal, and registrar-grade — and it exists to prove that a university department is a living system, and its software should behave like one.
