Overview · Shikho Campus AI

Shikho Campus AI

One brand, one budget, three modes: Tutor for the student, Assistant for the teacher, Analyst for the chairperson. The Coordinator has no AI mode.

Live demo Open the live campus demo and try Tutor, Assistant or Analyst. shikho-brac-platform.vercel.app ↗

What this product is

Shikho Campus AI is not three separate tools. It is one product that changes face by role. Every mode is grounded in real university material or records. Every mode is metered against a weekly cap that rolls up to the department. Every mode that can affect a student record requires a named human to approve before anything becomes real.

This page is the binding description of that product. The Student, Faculty and Administrator modules each own the screens where their mode appears; this page owns the rules that all three must obey, so a university buying the AI edition gets one coherent promise.

What this page owns The three modes, the rules that stay on, the usage model, the uniform Answer Card for Analyst, and the rule that AI always sits last in navigation. Engineering, product and a university buyer should be able to agree on this page without reading anything else about AI.

How it is built

Decision Campus AI is built in-house, using the same approach Shikho already proved in Teacher OS. We do not embed a third-party assistant product and we are not dependent on any external “copilot”. Shikho owns the retrieval, the prompts, the rules that stay on, the evaluation and the cost controls; the underlying language model is a replaceable component behind Shikho’s own service.

The reason to write this down is that “which AI do you use?” is the second question every university and every faculty senate asks. The answer must be the same in every meeting: it is ours, it is grounded in your material, and your content and student records never leave the platform’s control to train someone else’s product.

What this means for each mode

Tutor, Assistant and Analyst are not three integrations. They are one in-house engine pointed at three different sets of material with three different permissions. That is why the rules that stay on, the caps and the citations are worded once on this page and inherited by all three — and why replacing the underlying model later changes nothing a student, teacher or chair can see.

What must never happen

  • Campus AI must never send a university’s content or student records to an outside product to be trained on.
  • No mode may answer from the open internet instead of the institution’s own material.
  • Swapping the underlying model must never quietly turn off grounding, human approval, or the usage cap.

Usage and caps — AI you can afford

A procurement office’s first fear about AI is an unbounded bill. Every user has a weekly cap. Usage rolls up. The administrator sees the department total. Students are never cut off mid-lesson — when the cap is reached, the current answer finishes, then the surface explains the limit and when it resets. Grounded answers are designed to cost a fraction of open-ended generation. The story is “capped, not surprised.”

AI-TUTOR-01 · Tutor

P0

What it does. Tutor is the student’s mode of Shikho Campus AI. It is a course-scoped chat that answers only from that course’s published materials, syllabus and lecture transcripts. Every answer either cites where it came from or says the topic was not covered. It refuses to write graded work for the student. It is the same product as Assistant and Analyst — different altitude, same rules.

Why it exists

Students study at midnight on a phone. A tutor that invents an answer is worse than no tutor. A tutor that can only speak from the course the student is actually enrolled in is the only kind a computer-science department will trust, and the only kind that keeps the university’s licence bill predictable.

Who uses it and when

Student stuck on a recordingFrom Smart ClassAsks about the exact segment they are watching

What the user sees

Course picker

Only enrolled courses. The chat is always scoped to one course at a time.

“Asking about CSE110 · Programming Language I”
Chat

Student question and Tutor answer. Each answer shows its sources as short citations the student can open.

“From Week 4 lecture, 12:40–14:10 · and the syllabus §3”
Deferral

When the material does not cover the question.

“This is not covered in CSE110’s published material. Try asking about loops, arrays, or the Week 4 lab.”
rules that stay on

Always visible, not toggleable.

“Grounded answers only · Always on · Locked”
Refusal of graded work

“I can help you understand the topic. I cannot write the assignment for you.”

  4. 4
    SystemCounts the turn against the student’s weekly AI cap; if the cap is reached mid-answer, finishes the current answer then explains the limit.

Rules the university can set

  • Weekly Tutor cap per student, and whether unused capacity rolls over.
  • Whether Tutor may see only published materials, or also unpublished drafts the teacher has marked shareable with AI.
  • Whether conversation history is kept for the term or wiped more often.

What must never happen

  • Tutor must never invent a fact that is not in the course material.
  • Tutor must never write graded work or reveal another student’s answers.
  • Tutor must never pull material from a course the student is not enrolled in.
  • The Grounded answers only control must never become a toggle the student can switch off.

Unusual situations and how we handle them

  • Material has not been published yet. Tutor says the course has no published material yet, rather than guessing.
  • Two sections of the same course have different teachers. Tutor uses the materials of the section the student is enrolled in.
  • Cap reached mid-answer. The current answer finishes; the next question is blocked with a clear reset time — never cut off mid-lesson.

We know it works when

  • A chairperson can open any Tutor answer and see the citation that produced it.
  • A student asking something outside the course always gets a deferral, never a confident invention.
  • A request to “write my assignment” is refused in plain language every time.

AI-PRA-01 · Practice

P1

What it does. Practice is adaptive AI quizzing for the student. It targets the weakest topics first, using what the student has missed in earlier Practice runs and what Tutor conversations show they struggle with. Practice is not a graded exam — it is effort. Closing a Practice set contributes to the student’s private Momentum rings (Retrieve / Apply). Marks from Practice never enter the gradebook.

Why it exists

Practice and Tutor are the two student faces of Campus AI. Without Practice, Tutor is only chat. With Practice, the product can measure effort — consistency of study — without ever gamifying the grade.

Who uses it and when

PersonWhenWhat they achieve
StudentDaily revision, especially before quizzes and midtermsPractises the topics they are weakest on

What the user sees

Start Practice

Course picker and “practise my weak topics” as the default.

“CSE110 · Focusing on arrays and loops — your weakest topics this week”
Question card

One question at a time, drawn from course material; immediate explanation after answering, with a citation.

Session end

What improved, what is still weak, and a link back into Tutor for the weak topic.

“Retrieve ring closed for today · Practice never affects your grade”

How it works, step by step

  1. 1
    StudentOpens Student → Practice (or from Tutor / Momentum).
  2. 2
    SystemBuilds a short set aimed at weak topics from prior Practice and Tutor signals.
  3. 3
    StudentAnswers; sees an explanation with a citation after each item.
  4. 4
    SystemUpdates weak-topic map; closes the relevant Momentum ring if the day’s effort threshold is met; counts usage against the AI cap.

Rules the university can set

  • Whether Practice is on for a programme or course.
  • How many Practice questions count as closing a Momentum ring.
  • Whether teachers can see anonymised weak-topic heatmaps for their section (never individual Practice scores as grades).

What must never happen

  • Practice results must never appear in the gradebook or on a transcript.
  • Practice must never become a public leaderboard.
  • Practice must never invent questions from outside the course material.

Unusual situations and how we handle them

  • New student with no history. Practice starts from the current week’s published topics, not from a fake “weakness.”
  • Cap reached mid-set. Current question finishes; the set pauses with a clear resume time.

We know it works when

  • A student can show that Practice never changed a grade.
  • Weak topics in Practice match what the student actually struggles with in Tutor and in graded quizzes.

AI-SMART-01 · Smart Class

P1

What it does. Smart Class is the Tutor panel that sits beside a recorded lecture. It is grounded in the transcript of the segment the student is currently watching — not the whole internet, and not another course. The student can ask “what did the teacher just say about X?” and get an answer that cites the timestamp. Clips and a searchable transcript live on the same screen.

Why it exists

The recording player is a first-class surface: synced searchable transcript, clips, Smart Class AI panel, autoplay to the next lesson. Without Smart Class, a recording is only video. With it, a missed class becomes recoverable study time.

  • Every Smart Class answer shows a timestamp citation from the lecture being watched.

AI-AST-01 · Assistant

P0

What it does. Assistant is the teacher’s mode of Shikho Campus AI. It is grounded in the teacher’s own materials — slides, readings, recordings and transcripts. It drafts quizzes, lecture notes and announcements. Everything is a draft until the teacher edits and approves it. The notice “grounded — you approve” is always on and cannot be switched off.

Why it exists

Teachers will not use AI that publishes behind their back. They will use AI that saves them an hour of drafting and leaves the decision in their hands. That is the faculty half of “AI you can trust.”

AI-GRD-01 · Draft grading

P0

What it does. Inside the Faculty grading queue, AI can run automated checks (for example code tests) and draft a score and feedback. The surface states clearly: “The decision is always the teacher’s. AI drafts — no grade reaches a student without your approval.” States move needs_review → approved. The AI score is a draft; the teacher’s approval is the authoritative event.

Why it exists

This is the human-in-the-loop flagship. It is also the feature a sceptical faculty senate will interrogate first. The only durable answer is architectural: the AI is structurally incapable of publishing an unreviewed mark.

AI-ASK-01 · Analyst

P0

What it does. Analyst is the administrator’s mode of Shikho Campus AI — “Ask the department.” The chair asks a natural question and gets an answer computed from real records. Every answer uses one uniform Answer Card: headline, stat, ranked rows, source, drill, and note. Adding a new question is one compute function, never a bespoke screen. The engine is read-only, cites its source, and never invents. Ten starter intents cover the department’s real questions: worst attendance, lowest marks, top marks, at-risk students, weakest course, eligibility barred, certification pending, teacher load, term compare, department summary. A free-text scorer routes the question; no match returns a graceful deferral with suggestion chips.

Why it exists

Governance-grade intelligence without a report factory. The administrator asks, and the department’s own reality answers — instantly, grounded and cited.

AI-USE-01 · Usage and caps

P0

What it does. Makes AI affordable and predictable. Every Tutor, Practice, Smart Class, Assistant and Analyst turn counts against a weekly cap. The user sees their own meter. The administrator sees the department roll-up — the sum of every capped user — with the story “capped, not surprised.” Students and teachers are never cut off mid-lesson: the current answer finishes, then the limit is explained with the reset time.

Why it exists

Usage is a first-class surface because a procurement office’s first fear about AI is an unbounded bill. “AI you can afford” is half the pitch. Grounded answers are meant to cost a fraction of open-ended generation — the product should be designed so the useful path is also the cheap path.
