Introduction to the Administrator module

What this module is

The Administrator module is the governance layer of Shikho Campus. It is where a department chair, a dean, a controller of exams or a registrar delegate makes institutional decisions that other people must live by: this section’s marks are now official; these students may sit the final; this teacher owns this section; this attendance rule is the one we will lock; these unpaid dues block admit cards; this waiver is approved for finance to apply.

It is not a second gradebook, not a finance ledger, and not a clerical import desk. Those jobs belong to Faculty, to the university’s finance systems (mirrored through the Coordinator module), and to the Coordinator records desk. Administration answers a different question: is the institution running correctly, and what needs my decision before students are harmed by delay?

Who uses it

Primary users are academic officers — chairs and heads of department — working on a laptop in a department office in Dhaka, Chattogram or Sylhet, usually between meetings. Deans and school offices use wider views across departments. Registrar and exam-controller delegates use certification queues, eligibility locks, exam schedules and policy publishing. Quality assurance officers use outcome and evaluation reports before BAC or BAETE visits. None of these people should need a developer vocabulary to understand the screens.

A day in the life

It is a Sunday morning in result week. The chair of Computer Science opens Administrator → Decisions dashboard before the first meeting. Seven items are due today: four sections waiting for grade certification, one unstaffed laboratory section the coordinator flagged on Thursday, a merit-waiver case that must clear before billing lock, and an eligibility exception for a student with a hospital letter. She opens the first section under Administrator → Certify grades, reads the distribution and the flags, and certifies — she cannot type over a mark even if a parent is waiting outside. She returns one section to a teacher with a written reason about an unexplained fail concentration. She assigns a teacher to the laboratory under Administrator → Compose offering. She approves the waiver category for finance to price. She grants a time-bounded exam exception under Administrator → Exam eligibility and notes that the list will lock tomorrow evening. By lunch the dashboard is quieter, students who were blocked only by waiting signatures can move, and nothing on her screens has invented a fee or altered a teacher’s judgement.

ADM-HOME-01 · Decisions dashboard

P0

What it does. Gives the chair, dean or registrar one morning screen of decisions waiting — grades to certify, unstaffed sections, scholarship cases, disciplinary files, exam-duty gaps and fee-block exceptions — ranked by how soon they block students or the term calendar. One frame whose state changes by term phase (setup · current · closed), not three pages. Same three sections always: Needs you · Timeline · Performance. Needs you surfaces Certify, Eligibility, and Staff a section. An Ask bar opens Analyst.

Why it exists

At a Bangladeshi private university the calendar moves on hard deadlines: add-drop closes, eligibility locks, finals start, results must reach the registrar. Chairs and deans lose hours hunting email and spreadsheets for what needs a signature today. Without one decisions surface, urgent certification sits behind routine notices, a section opens without a teacher, and a student discovers a blocked admit card too late. The dashboard exists so governance work starts with urgency, not with browsing menus.

Rules the university can set

  • Which decision types appear for each role (chair vs dean vs registrar).
  • How many days before a deadline a row turns “due today” versus “due this week”.
  • Whether returned grade sections stay listed until the teacher resubmits.
  • Department scope: chair sees own department; dean may see a school; registrar may see institution-wide result queues.

What must never happen

  • The dashboard must never let anyone edit a mark from a shortcut — only open certify or return.
  • It must never invent a fee amount or imply the platform holds student money.
  • It must never hide a decision that blocks an admit card or registration without naming the owning office.
  • It must never show another department’s confidential disciplinary detail outside the viewer’s scope.

Unusual situations and how we handle them

  • If two chairs share a dual appointment, each sees only cases in their assigned scope.
  • If a decision is completed in another tab, the list refreshes so a second person does not act twice.
  • If the term has no open decisions, the calm empty state appears — not a blank page.
  • If the coordinator raises a new unstaffed flag, it appears on the next refresh.

We know it works when

  • A chair can clear a morning queue without opening unrelated menus.
  • Every row that blocks a student names the next action in plain language.
  • Returned certifications and unstaffed sections stay visible until resolved.
  • Walking from a dashboard row into the feature and back updates the count.

ADM-CRT-01 · Certify final grades

P0

What it does. Lets the chair review a section’s submitted finals — distribution, pass rate, flags — and either certify the whole section for the registrar or return it to the teacher with a written reason. The chair never changes a single mark.

Why it exists

A transcript line becomes permanent institutional truth. Teachers judge; chairs govern. Without a certify step, uneven distributions or incomplete columns reach the registrar unchecked. Without a hard ban on mark editing, chairs quietly “fix” numbers and destroy the audit trail. This feature inserts one governance gate — certify or return — and nothing else.

Who uses it and when

Teacher (indirect)When a section is returnedRead the reason and reopen the gradebook to correct and resubmit

Rules the university can set

  • Whether bulk certify is allowed, and only for unflagged sections.
  • Minimum length and required fields for a return reason.
  • Which statistical flags appear (high fail rate, skew, incomplete marks, outliers).
  • Whether students see finals immediately on certify or after a published result day.

What must never happen

  • The administrator must never edit, override, or type a mark — not even to correct a typo.
  • Certify must never accept a section with incomplete required columns if policy forbids it.
  • Return must never happen without a reason the teacher can read.
  • A certified section must never silently reopen for mark edits without a formal university reopening process.

Unusual situations and how we handle them

  • If the teacher is on leave when returned, the co-teacher or department office is notified from the staffing record.
  • If flags exist, bulk certify is blocked; the chair opens each flagged section.
  • If two chairs try to certify the same section, only the first succeeds.
  • If course evaluation must finish before results open, certify still records governance but student visibility waits on that rule.

We know it works when

  • No screen in this feature contains an editable mark field.
  • Every certified section appears in the coordinator’s registrar export list.
  • Every return lands in the teacher’s gradebook with the reason visible.
  • An auditor sees who certified which section and when — and never sees a mark changed by an administrator.

ADM-ELG-01 · Exam eligibility from attendance

P0

What it does. Computes who may sit midterms and finals from attendance, using the university’s percentage or band rules, locks the list before exams, and lets authorised officers grant documented exceptions.

Why it exists

Private universities in Bangladesh commonly bar students below an attendance threshold from exams. When eligibility lives in a spreadsheet, students argue at the door and exceptions vanish without a trail. This feature turns attendance into a clear eligible / barred / exception list before admit cards print, with a lock so last-minute lobbying cannot rewrite history unnoticed.

Rules the university can set

  • Attendance threshold percentage, or named bands with cut-offs.
  • Whether late arrival counts as present, partial, or absent.
  • How many days before the exam eligibility locks.
  • Who may grant exceptions (chair only, or also dean / controller of exams).
  • Whether an exception applies to midterm only, final only, or the whole term.

What must never happen

  • Eligibility must never be invented without attendance data or a recorded exception.
  • A locked list must never change because someone edited attendance quietly after the lock.
  • An exception must never alter the historical attendance percentage — only sitting permission.
  • Students must never see eligible status if they are barred and no exception exists.

Unusual situations and how we handle them

  • If a teacher corrects attendance before lock, eligibility recalculates automatically.
  • If correction arrives after lock, it queues as a formal unlock request — not a silent fix.
  • If a section never met, the system flags “insufficient sessions” instead of barring everyone at zero percent.
  • If sections are cross-listed, attendance follows the university’s cross-list counting rule.

We know it works when

  • Admit cards refuse barred students without a valid exception.
  • Lock timestamps and exception reasons are visible to auditors.
  • Near-threshold students can be listed for counselling before lock day.
  • A student barred in one section is not automatically barred in another without that section’s own result.

ADM-STF-01 · Compose offering and assign teachers

P0

What it does. Lets the chair build the term’s section offering and assign primary and secondary teachers. This is the only place teachers are assigned — coordinators load empty sections; chairs staff them.

An unstaffed section on day one is a student crisis and a reputation wound. Coordinators know the catalogue and the rooms; chairs know who can teach what. Separating record load from teacher assignment prevents a clerk from quietly placing a favourite teacher, and keeps academic judgement with the department head.

ADM-XL-01 · Cross-list sections

What it does. Lets the chair link two or more sections so they share one teaching space, one content set and one gradebook view while keeping separate official rosters for each catalogue line.

ADM-POL-01 · Academic policy settings

P0

What it does. Holds the university’s academic rules in one place: grade scale and letter bands, attendance rule, probation thresholds, credit limits per term, and retake caps — so every other feature enforces the same published truth.

Why it exists

When grade cut-offs live in a circular and attendance thresholds live in a chair’s head, students get contradictory answers from different offices. Policy settings exist so the catalogue year, the grade scale, probation and retake limits are configured once, versioned by term or catalogue, and readable by faculty, students and accreditation visitors alike.

Who uses it and when

ADM-FEE-01 · Fee policy and what unpaid dues block

P0

What it does. Defines which finance charge types exist for the university’s catalogue of fees, which unpaid dues create holds, and what those holds block — registration, admit cards, documents, graduation, and, where the university requires it, access to classes and course content — without ever taking or holding student money on the platform.

Decision — the tuition access gate In a private university, tuition is what makes the term real: a student who has not paid is not, in practice, taking the course. Shikho Campus supports that reality with a configurable access gate — unpaid tuition past its grace window can restrict live classes and new content, not only registration. It is designed to be firm but never a disaster: the student is warned well in advance, sees the exact amount and a one-tap way to pay, keeps read access to what they have already earned, and is never removed from a class that is in progress. A student who cannot pay is guided to an instalment, a waiver or the advisor — not dropped into a dead end.

Why it exists

Finance raises amounts in the university’s finance system; this feature only decides academic consequences of non-payment. Without a clear block map, one office blocks transcripts while another still prints admit cards. The platform never becomes a wallet: money moves from student to the university’s merchant accounts; Shikho Campus only mirrors dues and applies the hold rules the university set.

Unusual situations and how we handle them

  • If a scholarship is approved, the reduced due may auto-clear a tuition block.

  • If an instalment plan is active and on schedule, the access gate does not fire for the balance that is not yet due.
  • If the gate is due to fire mid-week, the student who opens a class gets a clear “access paused” screen with the amount and the pay action — not a silent empty course.

  • Unpaid tuition blocks exactly what the matrix says — no more, no less.
  • After verified payment, the matching hold disappears without a manual registrar phone call.
  • No screen implies a student wallet inside Shikho Campus.

Why it exists

Leaders ask the same questions every term and wait on someone to build a pivot table. A carefully scoped assistant speeds insight but must not become a way to edit marks, approve waivers, or message students in bulk by accident. Read-only is the product.

Rules the university can set

  • Whether answer logs are kept and for how long.
  • Which data domains the assistant may read.
  • Whether deans may query across departments.
  • Language preference (English / Bangla) for answers.

What must never happen

  • The assistant must never edit marks, certify sections, assign teachers, create fees, or approve scholarships.
  • It must never reveal another department’s confidential disciplinary detail outside scope.
  • It must never invent statistics not present in source data.
  • It must never imply the platform holds student money or can move funds.

Unusual situations and how we handle them

  • If data is incomplete (uncertified sections), the answer says so and excludes drafts.
  • If the question is out of scope, it refuses with a pointer to the right office or feature.
  • If entitlement for this assistant is off, the menu entry is hidden.

We know it works when

  • A chair gets a correct fail-rate answer that matches the certify reports.
  • A request to “set everyone to B+” is refused.
  • Links from answers land on the correct operational screens.
